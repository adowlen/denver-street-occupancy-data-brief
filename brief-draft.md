# Denver's street occupancy permits: the median permit runs 3 days, but 1.8 percent of segment permits hold 34.6 percent of all occupied time

**One-line finding:** Across 259,284 duration-valid segment records in Denver's street occupancy register (permits beginning 2017 to October 30, 2026), the median segment permit runs 3 days, while the 4,537 records running over 90 days (1.8 percent of records) account for 898,253 of the register's 2,595,960 segment-days (34.6 percent).

Source: City and County of Denver open data, street occupancy permits layer (ODC_DEV_STREETOCCUPANCYPPERMITS_L, 260,061 records pulled October 30, 2026), maintained by the Department of Transportation and Infrastructure (DOTI) from the Accela permit database and processed daily by Technology Services GIS. Crash figures join the register to Denver crash reports from January 1, 2021 to October 15, 2026 (Finding 9), with the method and its limits printed with the finding.

Three definitions govern everything below, and they are stated here rather than in a footnote. First, the unit of observation is the segment record, not the permit and not the closure: the service description states that a single permit may encompass multiple closures across several street segments and affect different portions of the street, so one permit produces several rows. Second, a segment-day is one segment record multiplied by its permitted duration in days (END_DATE minus BEGIN_DATE, as Denver calendar dates): it measures permitted time, not observed closure time, and a permit's recorded end date is a scheduled end, not a verified reopening. Third, durations are computed only for the 259,284 records whose dates pass the validity rules in Finding 2; every exclusion is counted there.

## Finding 1: The register holds 260,061 segment records under 97,538 permit numbers

The layer holds 260,061 records, pulled in full on October 30, 2026 (the server count and the local count are equal; the server count had been 259,891 the day before, and the service description states the data is processed daily). The records carry 97,538 distinct permit numbers, plus one record with no permit number. Rows per permit: median 2, maximum 94, and 44,294 permits (45.4 percent of permit numbers) hold exactly one row. Any count of "permits" taken from this layer's row count overstates the permit count by a factor of about 2.7, and any count of closures understates what the permits cover, because the layer does not say how many distinct closures a row represents.

The segment geometry behind the rows sums to 209,147,057 feet, or 39,611.2 miles of segment records. That figure counts the same physical street segment once for every permit record that covers it; it is a measure of permitted segment volume, not of unique street miles, and this brief never uses it as one. A stored point (X_COORD / Y_COORD) is present on 66,481 records (25.6 percent), and its presence varies sharply by begin year (79 percent of 2018 records, 6 percent of 2020 records, 99 percent of 2026 records in this pull), so no figure in this brief uses point proximity; the data notes record this as a deliberate exclusion.

## Finding 2: The raw dates run from 1902 to 2901; 777 records fall outside the duration window

BEGIN_DATE and END_DATE are recorded for nearly every record (3 records lack a begin date, 1 lacks an end date), but their raw ranges are not usable as printed: begin dates run from September 29, 1902 to December 11, 2901, end dates to December 30, 2099, and 83 records end before they begin. This brief maps implausible and missing dates to unknown and computes durations only inside a stated window: begin date between January 1, 2017 and the pull date October 30, 2026, end date present, end on or after begin, and end on or before December 31, 2027. The exclusions, which sum with the included records to the full pull, are:

- 16 records begin before 2017 (the earliest begin years in the register's bulk are 2017-2018; the layer's coverage effectively starts there).
- 665 records begin after the pull date. Of these, 650 are future-scheduled permits with valid end dates (the service description states the layer includes future scheduled permits), 13 begin after 2027, and 2 carry an end date a year before their begin date (begin April 21, 2027; end April 22, 2026).
- Inside the begin window, 68 records end before they begin and 25 end after the December 31, 2027 cap; none lacks an end date.

The window leaves 259,284 duration-valid records (99.7 percent of the pull). Every duration and segment-day figure below uses exactly this population and says so.

## Finding 3: Purpose and company are blank on essentially every 2018-2019 record

The register's PURPOSE and COMPANY fields have a vintage break. The share of duration-valid records with a recorded purpose, by begin year, is: 2018, 1 of 9,771 records (0.0 percent); 2019, 87 of 26,185 (0.3 percent); 2020, 23,972 of 35,852 (66.9 percent); 2021, 40,756 of 40,793 (99.9 percent); and 99.9 percent or better in each year from 2021 on. Company follows the same pattern (1 of 9,771 in 2018; 99.9 percent in 2021). In total, 47,775 duration-valid records carry neither a purpose nor a company, and nearly all of them begin before 2021.

The consequence is stated as a finding because it bounds what the public record can answer: no purpose or company comparison in this brief uses records beginning before 2021. Findings 6 and 7 use the 187,411 duration-valid records beginning in 2021 or later, of which 187,293 (99.9 percent) carry a purpose and 187,230 (99.9 percent) carry a company. Trends in Finding 5, which use dates and durations only, are unaffected by the break. What caused the break is not recorded in the layer's published description; this brief reports the population pattern and does not assign a cause.

## Finding 4: Occupied time concentrates in long permits

Across the 259,284 duration-valid records, permitted durations run: median 3 days, mean 10.0 days, 75th percentile 7 days, 90th percentile 18 days, 99th percentile 115 days, maximum 1,611 days. Same-day records (duration 0) number 39,933 (15.4 percent) and contribute no segment-days by this brief's definition. Summed over records, the register holds 2,595,960 segment-days in the window.

The distribution of segment-days across duration bands (segment records in each band, then that band's segment-days, both against the same 259,284-record population):

- 0 days: 39,933 records (15.4 percent); 0 segment-days (0.0 percent).
- 1-7 days: 156,505 records (60.4 percent); 473,323 segment-days (18.2 percent).
- 8-30 days: 47,440 records (18.3 percent); 715,219 segment-days (27.6 percent).
- 31-90 days: 10,869 records (4.2 percent); 509,165 segment-days (19.6 percent).
- 91-365 days: 4,163 records (1.6 percent); 687,330 segment-days (26.5 percent).
- Over 365 days: 374 records (0.1 percent); 210,923 segment-days (8.1 percent).

So the 4,537 records over 90 days (1.8 percent of records) hold 898,253 segment-days (34.6 percent), and the 374 records over a year hold 8.1 percent of all occupied time on 0.1 percent of records. Weighting by segment length instead of record count gives the same shape in a different unit: the window holds 2,437,479,405 foot-days of permitted occupancy (segment length in feet multiplied by duration in days, summed over the same records).

## Finding 5: Volume by year peaked in records in 2021 and in segment-days in 2023; the median permit lengthened from 2 days to 4

Segment records by begin year: 9,771 in 2018 (the register's first full year), 26,185 in 2019, 35,852 in 2020, a peak of 40,793 in 2021, 26,893 in 2022, 30,329 in 2023, 32,655 in 2024, 33,935 in 2025, and 22,806 in 2026 through October 30 (a partial year). Segment-days assigned to the begin year follow a different order: 347,885 in 2021, 327,835 in 2022, a peak of 382,831 in 2023, 370,933 in 2024, and 303,995 in 2025. The median duration rises from 2 days for 2018 and 2019 begins to 3 days in 2020-2021 and 4 days in each year from 2022 through 2025 (3 days for the partial 2026).

Two reading limits attach to this finding. Segment-days are assigned to the begin year, so a permit beginning in December 2025 and running into 2026 counts its full duration in 2025; the yearly figures are a begin-year accounting, not a calendar-year load. And because durations are as permitted at pull time, a recent year's segment-days can still change as permits are extended or shortened in the daily processing; 2026 is additionally partial and its median is computed on begins through October 30 only.

## Finding 6: By purpose, ROW Construction holds the most occupied time; Bike Parking holds the longest median (2021+ basis)

On the 2021+ basis of Finding 3 (187,293 records with a recorded purpose; shares below are of that population and of its segment-days), the largest purposes by records are Utility (34,645 records, 18.5 percent), Fiber Optics (25,465, 13.6 percent), and ROW Construction (22,166, 11.8 percent). By segment-days the order changes:

- ROW Construction: 22,166 records; 421,330 segment-days (21.9 percent of known-purpose segment-days); median 8 days.
- Utility: 34,645 records; 216,518 segment-days (11.3 percent); median 4 days.
- Safety: 2,599 records; 175,848 segment-days (9.2 percent); median 16 days.
- Street Cut - Water: 17,036 records; 150,532 segment-days (7.8 percent); median 5 days.
- Street Cut - Utilities: 17,037 records; 112,034 segment-days (5.8 percent); median 4 days.
- Miscellaneous - Multi-Categories: 3,996 records; 108,330 segment-days (5.6 percent); median 4 days.
- Fiber Optics: 25,465 records; 80,484 segment-days (4.2 percent); median 2 days.
- Bike Parking: 121 records; 76,881 segment-days (4.0 percent); median 585 days.

The register holds 44 recorded purpose values in the full pull. The contrast inside the table is the finding: Fiber Optics is the second-largest purpose by records and the second-smallest of these eight by occupied time, while Bike Parking, with 121 records, holds more segment-days than Fiber Optics on a median duration of 585 days. The register mixes short work permits with standing occupancies (bike parking corrals, valet, safety fencing around work sites) under one duration measure; Finding 4's concentration is partly this mixture, and the "What this might mean" section prints that alternative reading.

## Finding 7: By company string, Denver Water Board is largest; Xcel Energy appears under 179 different strings (2021+ basis)

On the same 2021+ basis (187,230 records with a recorded company, under 4,410 distinct company strings in 2021+ records), the largest exact strings by records are DENVER WATER BOARD (21,205 records; 207,816 segment-days; median 6 days), XCEL ENERGY-CENTRAL METRO (20,263 records; 95,099 segment-days; median 3 days), and PAULEY CONSTRUCTION LLC (10,397 records). By segment-days, the third-largest string is CCD (CPM Trans) & PCL (1,203 records; 56,048 segment-days), a joint city-and-contractor string.

The company field is free text, and it does not support a holder ranking beyond exact strings. The string XCEL appears in 179 distinct company strings on 2021+ records (37,451 records; 209,891 segment-days in total), including joint strings that name a city program and a contractor together, strings that name two companies together, and spelling and punctuation variants of the same operation. Summing those strings into one holder total would credit joint permits wholly to one party, so this brief does not compute holder totals; it reports that the register, as published, cannot produce them.

## Finding 8: Sidewalk classes hold 27.9 percent of segment-days; the top street is 16TH ST, driven by one long program

The portion of street occupied (ASIT_TYPE, populated on all but 238 duration-valid records, so this finding uses the full window) distributes segment-days as follows: 1st Lane holds the most records (54,234, 20.9 percent) and the most segment-days (461,970, 17.8 percent); Parking Lane holds 412,385 segment-days (15.9 percent); the sidewalk classes together (Sidewalk - Residential, Sidewalk - Collector/Arterial, Sidewalk - CBD/CC, and the covered-sidewalk classes) hold 57,837 records and 724,266 segment-days (27.9 percent); the full-closure classes (names containing "Full") hold 32,052 records and 306,751 segment-days (11.8 percent); Bike Lane holds 7,450 records and 51,344 segment-days (2.0 percent).

By the register's exact street names (1,562 distinct values in the window), the 10 highest streets by segment-days hold 11.3 percent of all segment-days. The top of the table: 16TH ST (1,200 records; 51,335 segment-days; median 3 days but mean 42.8 days), LARIMER ST (1,697 records; 42,928 segment-days), BLAKE ST (1,744; 28,525), E COLFAX AVE (2,364; 27,647, and the most foot-days of any street at 64,124,408), and N BROADWAY (1,781; 25,739). The 15TH ST value carries the most records of any street (2,389). On 16TH ST, the single largest company string is CCD (CPM Trans) & PCL with 378 of the 1,200 records; the gap between that street's median (3 days) and mean (42.8 days) is the signature of a small number of long program records among many short ones, the same concentration as Finding 4 at street scale. The full per-street panel is published with this brief (data/street_panel.csv).

## Finding 9: The streets carrying the most occupancy do not carry a matching share of crashes, and their crash severity share is not higher

Without this join, the register can say where permitted occupancy concentrates but nothing about whether that geography matches the crash geography the traffic-accidents brief measured. The join is by street name, and its construction bounds what it can say, so the method is part of the finding. Crash locations in the crash panel (106,121 reports, January 1, 2021 to October 15, 2026) are addresses: a block address names one street, an intersection address names two. A first-leg, exact-form parse of those addresses is not usable - it attributes 2 crashes to Broadway, while 2,856 crash addresses name Broadway as a block street or as either leg of an intersection, with an ST suffix the register does not use (the register's value is N BROADWAY). This brief therefore normalizes both sides (directional prefix and street-type suffix removed) and counts a crash as naming a street if the block street or either intersection leg matches a register street name. The full adjudication is in the package's audit record.

On that basis: 85,238 of the 106,121 crash reports (80.3 percent) name at least one street that appears in the occupancy register, producing 129,040 street mentions (an intersection crash counts once for each of its two streets). The 10 street-name groups with the most occupancy segment-days in 2021+ permits hold 14.2 percent of 2021+ segment-days and 10.3 percent of crash street mentions (13,240 of 129,040) - a smaller share of crash mentions than of occupied time. The severity comparison on the same mentions basis: 2.56 percent of the top-10 streets' mentions are KSI crashes (a crash with a death or a serious injury, crash-count basis), against 2.74 percent of all mentions. The two highest-mention street groups are COLFAX (4,387 mentions, 132 KSI) and BROADWAY (2,434 mentions, 64 KSI), each a group of the register's directional variants (E and W Colfax Ave; N and S Broadway) merged by the normalization.

Exposure caveat, stated prominently because it governs what this finding can mean: these are shares of street mentions and of segment-days, not rates. The sources carry no traffic-volume, pedestrian-volume, or street-length denominator matched to both datasets, normalized street names merge directional variants and street-type namesakes (16TH ST and 16TH AVE both normalize to 16TH), and a mention records where a crash was reported, not where any permit was active at that time - no record in either source establishes that a given crash occurred during a given permit's window on that street. The finding supports one statement only: at street-name scale, occupancy concentration and crash concentration are not the same geography, and the occupancy-heavy streets' crashes are not more severe by the KSI share.

## Finding 10: On the pull date, 631 segment records were active, and 650 future permits were already scheduled

A record is active on October 30, 2026 if its begin date is on or before that date and its end date is on or after it. On that basis the register holds 631 active segment records on the pull date, with a median total duration of 31 days, and 185 of the 631 are records whose total duration exceeds 90 days. The largest purposes among active records are ROW Construction (197), Street Cut - Water (75), and Street Cut - Utilities (63); the largest company strings are KRAEMER NORTH AMERICA LLC (54), DENVER WATER BOARD (43), and HOOPER CORPORATION (29). Separately, 650 future-scheduled records with valid dates begin after the pull date (Finding 2). These counts are a snapshot of one day in a daily-processed register; the active count in particular will differ on any other day, and this brief makes no claim about a typical day.

## What this might mean

One reading of Finding 4 is that the practical weight of street occupancy in Denver sits in a small long-duration cohort: 4,537 segment records over 90 days hold more than a third of all permitted occupied time, so any review, renewal, or coordination process aimed at that cohort addresses a third of the register's occupied time by touching 1.8 percent of its records. On that reading, the named streets and purposes in Findings 6 and 8 (ROW Construction; 16TH ST, where one joint city-and-contractor program string holds 378 of 1,200 records) are where that cohort is most visible in the public record.

The strongest alternative explanation is that a segment-day is permitted time, not occupied time. Permits are commonly taken out for a window that covers scheduling uncertainty, weather, and inspection waits, and long windows may stand open on segments where work is intermittent or finished early; the register records no actual start, stop, or reopening observation that could separate standing time from worked time. A second alternative runs alongside it: the register mixes two different kinds of occupancy - short work permits and standing uses with permit terms measured in years (Bike Parking, median 585 days; Valet - Long Term) - so part of the long-duration cohort is a standing-use inventory rather than long-running work, and the concentration figure blends the two. The deciding evidence would be an observed-occupancy field (actual closure dates) or a permit-class split between work permits and standing occupancies; neither exists in the published layer. This brief reports permitted time with those limits attached and makes no claim about days any street was actually blocked.

On Finding 3, the alternative explanation is a load-process change rather than a recording change at the permit counter: the register is an extract, and the layer's two GIS load types and its uneven point population by year show the extract's method has changed over the window. Either way, the published record before 2021 cannot support purpose or company statements, and this brief does not make any.

## Who can act

- **Denver Department of Transportation and Infrastructure (DOTI)**, which maintains the permits in Accela and owns the register's subject matter, controls how occupancy is summarized. Concrete action: publish a duration-based summary with the layer (segment-days by purpose, portion of street, and street, on the begin-year accounting this brief uses), so occupied time, not just permit counts, is the register's headline measure. Cost class: low-cost operational change (a derived summary from existing fields). Outcome measure: a published summary table whose totals reconcile to the layer's own counters, and this brief's Finding 4 figures (2,595,960 segment-days; 34.6 percent over 90 days) rerun against it on the next pull.
- **DOTI with Denver's open data program (Technology Services / DenverGIS)**, publisher of the layer, controls what the published record can support. Concrete actions: state in the layer's documentation why PURPOSE and COMPANY are unpopulated for permits beginning before 2021, or backfill the fields from Accela if the values exist there; and add a normalized holder identifier alongside the free-text COMPANY string, so joint and variant strings can be grouped without crediting joint permits to one party. Cost class: low-cost operational change (documentation and a derived field). Outcome measure: the purpose-known share by begin year on the next pull (0.0 percent for 2018 and 0.3 percent for 2019 in this pull), and a distinct-string count for Xcel-named strings (179 on 2021+ records in this pull) that resolves to a stated holder grouping.
- **DOTI's right-of-way program**, which issues and renews the permits, controls the long-duration cohort's terms. Concrete action: a stated renewal review for segment permits running over 90 days - the 4,537 records holding 34.6 percent of segment-days - confirming the segment is still occupied or closing the record. Cost class: budget decision (a standing review is staff time across thousands of records, not a data edit). Outcome measure: the over-90-day share of segment-days and the count of records over 365 days (374 in this pull) on the next register pull, computed on this brief's window rules.
- **Denver City Council**, in its budget process (the 2027 budget amendment vote is November 2 and passage November 9), controls DOTI's program funding. Concrete action: where right-of-way and street-work funding is weighed, weigh it against an occupied-time baseline - segment-days by purpose (ROW Construction 421,330 on the 2021+ basis) and the over-90-day cohort's share - rather than permit counts alone, which rank Fiber Optics second and Bike Parking near-last while occupied time ranks them in the opposite order. Cost class: budget decision. Outcome measure: a baseline tabled in the budget process that this package's stats file can be checked against.

## Data notes

- Snapshot and vintage: all figures are from the pull of October 30, 2026 (260,061 records; server count and local count equal). The service reports its data last edited October 30, 2026 (04:45 MDT), and the service description states the data is processed daily from Accela, so the register is a moving extract: the server count was 259,891 on October 29. Figures describe the register as pulled, not any earlier or later state of it.
- Dates: ArcGIS date fields arrive as epoch milliseconds and are converted to America/Denver calendar dates before any arithmetic. Duration is end minus begin in calendar days; a same-day permit has duration 0 and contributes 0 segment-days (39,933 such records). The window and caps are stated in Finding 2; begin dates in 1902-2016 and after 2027 are sentinel or erroneous values in a small tail (16 and 13 records), printed rather than silently dropped.
- Unit discipline: rows are segment records throughout. Permit counts use distinct PERMIT_NUMBER values (97,538, plus 1 null). Segment length sums count physical segments once per covering record (Finding 1). Crash figures in Finding 9 are street mentions on the stated basis, never crash locations matched to permits.
- Vintage break: PURPOSE and COMPANY population by begin year is printed in Finding 3 and in stats.json; every purpose and company figure states its 2021+ basis. ASIT_TYPE is populated across the window (239 null in the full pull) and its figures use the full window. EXEMPT is populated on 72,330 records but is used by no figure in this brief; its server-side grouping does not reproduce the local counter (the audit record adjudicates this), which is a further reason it carries no finding.
- Joins: the only join is the crash street-name join in Finding 9 (match rate 80.3 percent of crash reports naming at least one register street; the rejected first-leg method and its Broadway result are recorded in the audit). No point-proximity join is used, because the layer's stored point is present on only 25.6 percent of records with a year-varying pattern (Finding 1). No parcel, neighborhood, or street-network join is used; street figures are register street-name groups, and the layering admission for the crash layer is printed in Finding 9.
- Category stability: the PURPOSE and ASIT_TYPE value lists were counted in full before any figure was computed (44 purpose values and 23 ASIT values in the pull, plus nulls, recorded in the audit output); no category renaming inside the 2021+ basis window was detected in those counters, and the brief's categories are the register's own strings, printed verbatim.
- Layering test: the crash layer is what lets this brief compare occupancy geography against an observed outcome (Finding 9); the segment-length field is what lets it restate occupied time weighted by segment size (foot-days, Finding 4). Each layer's claim is stated with its finding. A neighborhood layer was considered and cut: the register carries no neighborhood field and its point population is too uneven by year to assign records to neighborhoods without bias, so no neighborhood claim is made.
- Reproduction: code/pull_data.py records the full pull and the server counters; code/build_stats.py builds stats.json and data/street_panel.csv; code/audit_section10.py records the pre-flight audit (output in data/audit_output.txt); code/verify_independent.py is the independent recomputation behind verification-ledger-independent.md; code/charts.py draws the charts; code/hero_composite.py composites the hero.
